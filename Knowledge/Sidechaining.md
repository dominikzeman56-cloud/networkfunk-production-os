# Sidechaining

---

## Core Principle
> Create space, don't kill dynamics. Sidechain for clarity, not for pumping.

---

## Sidechain Types

### Frequency-Specific
- Duck only specific frequencies
- Preserve transients
- Bass freq: 40-200 Hz
- Full range: 20 Hz - 20 kHz

### Multiband
- Duck only problem frequencies
- Keep lows intact
- Control mask at specific points
- Most surgical approach

### Ducking
- Reduce entire signal
- Visible pumping effect
- More aggressive
- Common in EDM

---

## Parameters

### Threshold
- Higher: more ducking
- Lower: less ducking
- Set by ear

### Ratio
- 2:1-4:1: subtle
- 4:1-8:1: moderate
- 8:1+: extreme

### Attack
- Fast (0.1-5 ms): instant ducking
- Medium (5-20 ms): some transient through
- Slow (20-50 ms): pumps after transient

### Release
- Fast (50-150 ms): quick recovery
- Medium (150-300 ms): natural pump
- Slow (300+ ms): lazy pump

### Range
- How much ducking (-1 to -24 dB)
- -3 to -6 dB: typical
- -6 to -12 dB: aggressive

---

## Common Applications

### Kick → Bass
- Frequency-specific
- Duck 40-200 Hz
- Attack: 0.1-1 ms
- Release: 50-100 ms
- Range: -3 to -6 dB

### Kick → Pad
- Full range or frequency
- Attack: 1-5 ms
- Release: 150-300 ms
- Range: -6 to -12 dB

### Snare → Bass
- Frequency-specific
- Duck 100-300 Hz
- Attack: 0.1-1 ms
- Release: 50-100 ms

### Kick → Full Mix
- Multiband or full range
- Subtle pumping effect
- Release matches tempo

---

## Tips

1. Frequency-specific sidechain preserves sub
2. Match release to tempo (roughly 1/4 or 1/2 note)
3. Less is usually more
4. Test in mono, listen in stereo
5. Sidechain for clarity, not style
6. Fast attack for tight ducking
7. Slow release for smoother pump

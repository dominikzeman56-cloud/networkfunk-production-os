# EQ

---

## Core Principle
> Make space for each element. Cut first, boost second.

---

## EQ Types

### Subtractive
- Remove problem frequencies
- Fix issues first
- Cleanest approach

### Additive
- Enhance character
- Add positives after subtractive
- Creative approach

### Dynamic
- Frequency-specific compression
- GLS/EQs
- Problem frequency control

---

## Frequency Map

| Range | Hz | Content | Priority |
|-------|-----|---------|----------|
| Sub | 20-60 | Fundamental | 1 |
| Low | 60-120 | Weight | 2 |
| Lower-Mid | 120-250 | Mud | 3 |
| Mid | 250-500 | Body | 4 |
| Upper-Mid | 1k-4k | Presence | 5 |
| High | 4k-8k | Clarity | 6 |
| Air | 8k-16k | Brilliance | 7 |

---

## Cut First

### High-Pass Filter
- Kick: below 30 Hz
- Bass: below 30 Hz
- Snare: below 50 Hz
- Hats: below 200 Hz
- Pads: below 50 Hz
- Everything else: below 30-50 Hz

### Problem Frequencies
- Mud: 200-400 Hz
- Boxy: 400-800 Hz
- Nasal: 1-2 kHz
- Harsh: 3-5 kHz
- Sibilant: 5-8 kHz

---

## Boost Second

### Typical Boosts
- Kick body: 50-80 Hz
- Kick attack: 3-5 kHz
- Snare crack: 3-5 kHz
- Bass body: 100-200 Hz
- Vocals: 2-5 kHz
- Air: 10-16 kHz

### Boost Guidelines
- Small boosts (2-4 dB)
- Wide Q for gentle curves
- Narrow Q for specific peaks

---

## EQ Order

### Insert Order (typical)
1. High-pass filter
2. Cut problem frequencies
3. Dynamic EQ (if needed)
4. Boost character frequencies
5. Air/high boost

### Parallel Processing
- Multiple EQs for different purposes
- Clean EQ for subtractive
- Colored EQ for additive

---

## Tips

1. Cut first, boost second
2. Make space, don't just boost
3. High-pass everything except kick and bass sub
4. Small boosts, small cuts
5. Q width: narrow for surgery, wide for shaping
6. Listen in context, not solo
7. If you can't hear the difference, don't do it

# Distortion

---

## Core Principle
> Distortion adds edge and aggression. Use it purposefully, not as default.

---

## Distortion Types

### Overdrive
- Soft clipping
- Tube/amp character
- Best for: warmth, edge
- Character: musical, smooth

### Distortion
- Hard clipping
- Aggressive harmonics
- Best for: aggression, impact
- Character: harsh, forward

### Fuzz
- Extreme clipping
- Square wave-ish
- Best for: texture, accents
- Character: raw, fuzzy

### Bitcrush
- Reduced bit depth
- Digital distortion
- Best for: lo-fi texture
- Character: digital, crunchy

### Wavefolding
- Waveform folding
- Odd harmonics
- Best for: metallic sounds
- Character: glassy, harsh

---

## Distortion Chain

### Pre-Filter
- Filter > Distortion
- Darker distortion
- More body, less harsh

### Post-Filter
- Distortion > Filter
- Brighter distortion
- More edge, more harsh

### Mid-Side
- Independent M/S distortion
- Control width under distortion
- Bass mono, sides distorted

---

## Common Applications

### Kick
- Light overdrive
- 5-10% drive
- Adds click and attack

### Snare
- Medium distortion
- 10-20% drive
- Adds edge and power

### Bass
- Overdrive for warmth
- 10-15% drive
- Makes bass sit in mix

### Lead
- Distortion for edge
- 15-30% drive
- Adds presence, aggression

### FX
- Heavy distortion
- 30-50%+ drive
- For risers, impacts, transitions

---

## Tips

1. Distortion is a tool, not an effect
2. Use overdrive for warmth, distortion for edge
3. Filter before heavy distortion
4. Parallel blend for control
5. Check mono compatibility
6. Bitcrush for texture, not bass
7. Use for accents, not constant

# Delay

---

## Core Principle
> Delay creates movement and space. Use it to enhance rhythm, not compete with it.

---

## Delay Types

### Ping-Pong
- Alternates left/right
- Best for: stereo width, movement
- Rhythm: 1/8, 1/16 notes

### Stereo
- Independent left/right
- Best for: wide pads, atmosphere
- Rhythm: varies

### Tempo-Sync
- Synced to project tempo
- Best for: rhythmic effects
- Dotted 1/8, 1/16, 1/4

### Random
- Irregular pattern
- Best for: experimental, texture
- Less predictable

---

## Parameters

### Time
- Dotted 1/8: rhythmic bounce
- 1/16: tight, rhythmic
- 1/4: space between repeats

### Feedback
- Low (20-40%): subtle echo
- Medium (40-70%): building repeats
- High (70%+): infinite, runaway

### Mix
- Dry/Wet control
- Low: 10-30% for subtle
- Medium: 30-60% for obvious
- High: 60-100% for effect

### Filter
- Low-pass: dark echoes
- High-pass: click/percussive only
- Band: specific character

---

## Common Uses

### Rhythmic Delay
- Time: 1/8 or 1/16
- Feedback: 30-50%
- Filter: High-pass
- Mix: 20-40%
- Best for: hats, snare, leads

### Space Delay
- Time: 1/4 or dotted 1/8
- Feedback: 40-60%
- Filter: Low-pass
- Mix: 30-50%
- Best for: atmosphere, pads

### Dub Delay
- Time: 1/4
- Feedback: 50-70%
- Filter: Low-pass with modulation
- Mix: 40-60%
- Best for: bass, dub-style FX

### Reverse Delay
- Time: varies
- Feedback: low
- Filter: varies
- Mix: subtle
- Best for: builds, transitions

---

## Tips

1. High-pass before delay (except bass if intended)
2. Tempo-sync for rhythmic delay
3. Use feedback for building tension
4. Filter echoes to prevent mud
5. Sidechain delay for clarity
6. Mono delay before stereo processing
7. Use for movement, not decoration

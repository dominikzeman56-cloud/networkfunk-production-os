# Saturation

---

## Core Principle
> Add harmonics, not distortion. Saturation warms, distortion aggresses.

---

## Saturation Types

### Tape
- Soft clipping
- Warm harmonics
- Best for: gentle warmth, glue
- Character: smooth, musical

### Tube
- Asymmetric clipping
- Rich harmonics
- Best for: warmth, character
- Character: smooth, colorful

### Transformer
- Iron saturation
- Limited harmonic content
- Best for: subtle thickness
- Character: tight, focused

### Clip
- Hard clipping
- Odd harmonics
- Best for: edge, aggression
- Character: harsh, forward

---

## Saturation Chain

### Instrument Level
- Light saturation (2-10%)
- Adds character to source
- Before compression

### Bus Level
- Gentle saturation (2-5%)
- Glues elements together
- Before or after compression

### Master Level
- Very light (1-3%)
- Final polish
- After limiter

---

## Common Applications

### Kick
- Tape-style
- 5-15% drive
- Adds weight and punch

### Snare
- Tube or tape
- 10-20% drive
- Adds crack and body

### Bass
- Tape or tube
- 5-15% drive
- Adds warmth, makes bass sit

### Drums Bus
- Tape
- 3-8% drive
- Glues drums together

### Master Bus
- Tape or tube
- 1-3% drive
- Final warmth polish

---

## Gain Staging with Saturation

1. Input at -18 dBFS
2. Increase saturation
3. Adjust output to match input
4. Check for clipping
5. Parallel blend if too much

---

## Tips

1. Less is more with saturation
2. Tape/tube for warmth, clip for edge
3. Check transients after saturation
4. Use parallel for heavy saturation
5. High-pass before heavy saturation
6. Saturation after compression for gloss
7. Saturation before compression for punch
